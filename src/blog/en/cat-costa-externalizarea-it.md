"How much does it cost to outsource my IT?" is the first question any business owner asks — and, unfortunately, the one whose honest answer is "it depends." But "it depends" doesn't mean you can't estimate. It means the price is built from a few clear factors you can assess yourself before asking for a quote.

This article explains why the cost varies, which pricing models exist, and what ballpark prices to expect, so you walk into a conversation with a provider already knowing what's reasonable.

## What the price depends on

Four factors set the cost of a managed IT service:

- **The number of users.** The most common pricing criterion. More people = more support, more accounts, more devices to secure.
- **The number and type of devices.** Workstations, laptops, servers, printers, network gear. A server needs far more attention than a workstation.
- **The level of security you want.** Basic support costs little. Real managed security — EDR, monitoring, immutable backup, hardening — costs more, but it's exactly the part that saves you during an attack.
- **The SLA (guaranteed response times).** A 15-minute response to critical incidents costs more than "we'll get to it when we can." You pay for availability.

## Pricing models

| Model | How it works | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Per user / month | A fixed price per employee, regardless of how many devices they have | Companies where people use 1-2 devices — the most predictable |
| Per device / month | A price per workstation, server, device | Companies with many devices per person or many servers |
| Fixed monthly package | One global subscription for the whole infrastructure | Companies that want a single, simple number within budget |
| Break-fix (hourly) | You pay only when something breaks | **Avoid it** — it discourages prevention and leaves you exposed |

The **break-fix** model looks cheap, but it's a trap: the provider earns when things break, not when they work. For a company that wants stability and security, a monthly subscription (per user or fixed package) is almost always the right choice.

## Ballpark prices

The figures below are indicative and vary a lot by security level and SLA. We give them as a reference, not a quote (Romanian market):

- **Basic support** (helpdesk, light administration): roughly 50–100 RON / user / month.
- **Full managed IT with security** (support + network + EDR + backup + monitoring + SLA): roughly 120–250 RON / user / month.
- **Servers and critical infrastructure**: priced separately, by number and role.

For a 20-employee company, a full managed service sits, indicatively, somewhere around 2,500–5,000 RON / month — that is, **below the cost of a single in-house IT person** with salary, taxes and holidays, but with access to a whole team of specialists.

## What must never be missing from the price

A cheap subscription that doesn't include these isn't a saving, it's a deferred risk:

- **Immutable, tested backup** — without it, ransomware stops your business no matter how good the rest is.
- **Real managed security** — not just an antivirus, but EDR, monitoring and incident response.
- **A written SLA** — guaranteed response times, not verbal promises.
- **Monthly reports** — so you know what you're paying for and what state your infrastructure is in.

For companies that fall under [NIS2](/en/nis2/), these components aren't optional — they're exactly the requirements an auditor checks.

## Conclusion

The price of IT outsourcing isn't a fixed number, but it's predictable once you know how many users you have, which devices, what level of security and what SLA. The golden rule: beware of very cheap offers that "forget" about backup, security and SLA — that's where the real cost hides, and you pay it at the first incident.
