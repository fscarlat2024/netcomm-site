When you move a company to Microsoft 365, the first important decision is the licence. And here most people get it wrong in two directions: they either pick a licence that's too expensive for what they need, or one that's too cheap and leaves security out. The two plans that cause the most confusion are **Business Premium** and **E3**.

This article explains the real difference between them — not the feature list on Microsoft's website, but what matters for a practical decision.

## The difference in short

On paper, both include Office, Exchange, Teams, SharePoint and OneDrive. The real difference is in **security** and in the **user limit**:

| | Business Premium | E3 |
|---|---|---|
| Office + Exchange + Teams + SharePoint | Yes | Yes |
| Security included (Defender, Intune) | **Yes, complete** | Partial — components missing |
| Microsoft Defender for Business / Endpoint | Yes (for Business) | No (needs an add-on or E5) |
| Intune (device management) | Yes | Yes |
| Conditional Access / Entra ID P1 | Yes | Yes |
| User limit | **Max 300** | No limit |
| Positioning | SMBs | Large / enterprise companies |

## Why Business Premium is the right choice for most SMBs

For a company under 300 users, **Business Premium offers the best value**, for a reason many people miss: it includes the security you need, without add-ons.

Business Premium comes with Microsoft Defender for Business (anti-malware and EDR protection for endpoints), Intune (device management) and Conditional Access (condition-based access control). In other words, in a single licence you get exactly the ingredients you need to secure accounts and devices — including for [NIS2](/en/nis2/) compliance.

E3, by contrast, is designed for large companies. It has enterprise features (no 300-user limit, advanced retention, extended compliance), but — surprisingly — **it doesn't include the same endpoint security** as Business Premium. For full security on E3, you need add-ons or an upgrade to E5, which significantly increases the cost.

## When you need E3 (or E5)

E3 makes sense when:

- You exceed **300 users** — above this threshold, the Business plans are no longer available.
- You have advanced compliance needs, legal retention (eDiscovery, retention policies) or long-term archiving.

**E5** makes sense when you want top-tier security and compliance: Defender for Endpoint Plan 2, Entra ID P2 (PIM, Identity Protection), full Microsoft Purview, PSTN calling. It's the most expensive plan and is justified at organizations with serious security requirements.

## The common trap

The most frequent mistake: a 40-person company takes E3 "because it sounds more enterprise", then discovers it has no endpoint protection and must pay for add-ons on top. The result: they pay more than Business Premium, for less out-of-the-box security.

The simple rule: **under 300 users, start from Business Premium.** Move to E3/E5 only if you have a concrete requirement that forces it (scale, advanced compliance).

## Conclusion

The choice isn't "which is more expensive", but "which gives you the security you need without add-ons". For most small and mid-sized companies in Romania, Business Premium is the answer: complete security, device management and compliance, in a single licence, under 300 users.
