Every growing company reaches the same question: do I hire an IT person or outsource? The right answer isn't universal — it depends on the company's size, the complexity of the infrastructure and how critical technology is to the business. But it's a decision you can make rationally, not on reflex.

This article compares the two options honestly, with the upsides and the traps of each, so you choose with your eyes open.

## The single in-house IT person problem

The most common situation at small and mid-sized companies: one person who "handles IT." It looks cheap and simple, but it has three structural problems:

- **They can't know everything.** Network, security, cloud, backup, servers, telephony — these are different fields, each with its own specialization. One person does them all at an average level and none at an expert level.
- **They're a single point of failure.** When they're on holiday, sick or leave, the company is left without IT. And they often leave with all the passwords and knowledge in their head.
- **They cost more than it seems.** Salary, taxes, holidays, training, equipment — plus the idle time when there's nothing to do.

## The comparison, in short

| Criterion | In-house (1 person) | Outsourced (provider) |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | Salary + taxes + benefits | Fixed subscription, usually lower |
| Skill coverage | One person, average across the board | A team, experts in each area |
| Availability | Working hours, one person | Extended coverage, guaranteed SLA |
| Continuity | Risk on holiday/departure | No single point of failure |
| Scaling | Hire another person | Grow the subscription, instantly |
| Knows the business | Very well, day to day | Well, but less intimately |

## When in-house IT makes sense

Outsourcing isn't the answer for everyone. An in-house team makes sense when:

- The company is large (hundreds of users) and justifies a full team, not a single person.
- You have very specific software or processes that require daily internal knowledge.
- Technology is your product, not just a support tool.

Even then, many choose a **hybrid model**: an internal team for what's specific to the business + an external provider for security, backup and infrastructure, areas where external specialization beats an internal generalist.

## When outsourcing makes sense

For most companies of 10–100 employees, outsourcing wins for simple reasons:

- **Access to a team, at the price of one person.** Network, security, cloud, backup — each with its own specialist.
- **Predictable cost.** A fixed subscription, no surprises and none of the hidden costs of hiring.
- **No single point of failure.** Nobody goes on holiday with the whole infrastructure in their head.
- **Compliance readiness.** A serious provider builds everything aligned with [NIS2](/en/nis2/) requirements, useful at audit.

## Conclusion

In-house vs outsourced IT isn't a question of principle, but of scale and risk. Below a certain size, a single in-house IT person is a risky bet: expensive for what they cover and fragile when they disappear. Above a certain scale, an internal team becomes justified — often complemented by a provider for the specialized areas.
